Abstract

Tabular surrogates are commonly ranked on random validation splits. Yet a surrogate is used to predict wherever no simulation exists, both between training points (interpolation) and beyond the training domain (extrapolation), and random splits cannot tell the two apart, so the selected model may fail where it extrapolates. We introduce FLOATBench, a tabular dataset and benchmark for fatigue prediction on floating offshore wind turbine towers that tests whether model selection stays valid as predictions move from interpolation to extrapolation. It provides 582,120 per-section fatigue-damage labels from 19,404 OpenFAST simulations of three 22 MW tower geometries over 1,078 wind–wave operating conditions.

FLOATBench is distinguished by a regime-aware partition that places each test point relative to the training domain as in-train, interpolation, or extrapolation, so extrapolation depth is controlled by construction, and by cross-tower transfer with condition-level bootstrap intervals. Across 735 trained surrogates and seven standalone baselines on three towers, the global rank-1 model falls to ranks 23, 11, and 11 in joint wind-and-wave extrapolation, where a network ranked 69th–79th globally wins, and validation-based selection leaves 1.5–1.9× the best available extrapolation error. In cross-tower transfer, Rel L² error is 0.067–0.098 when the reference tower is in training versus 0.423 when it is held out.

Robustness checks show that the crossover holds across all non-degenerate partition settings and after retraining on two alternative training grids. Limitations: one 22 MW design family and aligned power-production conditions. Resources: dataset, code, and trained surrogates are released.

Dataset

Operating envelope. Each tower is simulated across a 22 × 7 × 7 grid of environmental conditions: 22 mean hub-height wind speeds at the midpoints of 1 m/s bins from cut-in (3 m/s) to cut-out (25 m/s), and, for each wind speed, seven significant wave heights Hs with seven peak periods Tp per Hs, sampled from the joint wind–wave operating distribution used in FLOAT. This yields 49 sea states per wind speed and 1,078 unique (wind, wave) operating points per tower. Wind and waves are aligned.

Simulation setup. All simulations follow IEC 61400-3-2 design load case (DLC) 1.2 (normal power production) and run in OpenFAST. Each operating point is simulated with 6 independent turbulence seeds, giving 6,468 ten-minute simulations per tower; runs last 1,000 s with the final 600 s post-processed. Each run records 88 time series at 10 Hz (turbine, tower and platform outputs), about 190 GB across the 19,404 runs of the three towers, at roughly one CPU-hour per run.

Damage labels. For each operating condition and each of the 30 tower sections, the bending-moment time series is converted to stress, rainflow-counted and Miner-summed against the DNV-RP-C203 S–N curve E, giving the cumulative damage D, the released label. The load-equivalent transform DEL ∝ D1/m with m = 3 is used as the regression target.

Tower geometries. Three towers share the same three-column semi-submersible platform, controller and mooring. REF is the IEA-22-280-RWT baseline tower, designed for fixed-bottom conditions without explicit fatigue constraints (D ≈ 32 at the base, about 9 months of life under Miner's rule on a 25-year horizon). OPT1 is an intermediate fatigue-aware redesign produced by FLOAT (D from 0.49 to 1.28 along the height), and OPT2 the final iterate targeting D ≤ 0.90 (labels from 0.78 to 0.94). The redesigns change the outer diameter and wall thickness at constant tower height (149.386 m). Each tower is discretised into 30 sections, with damage labels at the section midpoints: 194,040 rows per tower and 582,120 in total.

FLOATBench OpenFAST outputs and damage pipeline. Each simulation yields 88 time series at 10 Hz around the IEA-22-280-RWT on a three-column semi-submersible: simulations → time series → bending moments → stress → rainflow → S–N + Miner → damage labels.

Terms used on this page

Wind speed VMean wind speed at hub height, in m/s. The 22 levels are the midpoints of 1 m/s bins between cut-in (3 m/s) and cut-out (25 m/s): 3.5, 4.5, …, 24.5 m/s. Its standard deviation over the 10 minutes is used as a proxy of turbulence intensity.
Wave height HsSignificant wave height, in m: roughly the average height of the highest third of the waves. It measures how rough the sea is.
Wave period TpPeak wave period, in s: the period of the waves that carry the most energy. Long periods mean long swell, short periods a choppy sea.
Turbulence seedOne random realisation of turbulent wind for the same mean conditions. Each condition is simulated with 6 seeds, so the same U, Hs, Tp gives 6 slightly different results.
Tower sectionOne of the 30 slices of the tower where fatigue is evaluated: section 1 is the base, section 30 the top.
Fatigue damage DFraction of the fatigue life consumed (Miner's rule). Per simulation it is the damage of 600 s; summed over 25 years with damage_weight, D = 1 means the section reaches its fatigue limit.
DELDamage equivalent load: the constant-amplitude load that would cause the same damage, DEL ∝ D1/3. Surrogates are trained and scored on this target.
Rel L² DELRelative L² error of the predicted DEL, ‖prediction − truth‖ / ‖truth‖ over the test rows: the primary ranking metric (0.05 is about 5% error; lower is better). MRE, the point-wise mean relative error, is the per-regime metric.
RegimesWhere a test point lies relative to the training data, labelled separately for wind and for waves: In-train (IT) close to training points, Interpolate (IP) inside the training region but between points, Extrapolate (EX) outside it. A cell such as EX_IT reads wind label first, wave label second; EX_EX is extrapolation in both wind and waves, the hardest case.
E1 / E2 / E3The three protocols: random split (E1), regime-aware split within one tower (E2), and training on two towers and testing on the third (E3).

Benchmark Protocol

Regime-aware partition

Train/test split. From the 22 × 7 × 7 grid, test holds the extreme and middle indices on both the wind and wave axes, leaving 18 of 22 wind speeds and a 4 × 4 inner wave patch on the train side. All 6 seeds of a condition stay in the same fold, to prevent train–test leakage. This yields 51,840 train and 142,200 test rows per tower (about 27/73). Unlike an i.i.d. 80/20 split, the partition induces a structured covariate shift along both axes: the test set is larger than the training set and lies outside its support, so every extrapolation region is densely sampled.

Regime labelling. Each test point is labelled In-train, Interpolation or Extrapolation (IT, IP, EX) independently in the wind (mean V, std V) and wave (Hs, Tp) subspaces:

  1. Train spacing. The mean nearest-neighbour distance between training points, in standardised units, sets the spacing scale.
  2. Distance-based grouping. A test point whose nearest-training distance, normalised by that scale, is at most 0.5 is IT; the rest are provisionally IP.
  3. Boundary override. A concave hull (alpha shape, α = 0.1) bounds the training support. Test points outside the hull and at least a tolerance ε from its boundary (0.049 in the wind plane, 0.021 in the wave plane) become EX; points just outside but within the tolerance stay IP.

Crossing the two labels gives nine joint regimes, with EX_EX (extrapolation on both axes) the worst case. A random split samples test points from the training envelope and leaves every extrapolation regime empty; the regime-aware partition populates all nine. The grid counts test simulations per cell (the same for every tower).

FLOATBench regime partition in the wind (left) and wave (right) subspaces; the training domain (alpha-shape hull) is shaded.

Evaluation levels

E1 · Random split

A random partition matched in size to E2 (about 27/73), run on each tower. Same surrogates and training-set size as E2, so any difference reflects the partitioning alone.

E2 · Within tower

The regime-aware partition, run on each tower. Results per regime (EX_EX as worst-case extrapolation) and per section (section 1 base vs section 30 top).

E3 · Cross tower

Leave one tower out: train on two towers, test on the third (REF+OPT1 → OPT2, REF+OPT2 → OPT1, OPT1+OPT2 → REF), with the operating envelope fixed.

Surrogates. XGBoost, LightGBM, CatBoost, NeuralNetFastAI, NeuralNetTorch, ExtraTrees, RandomForest and TabM, trained through AutoGluon-Tabular 1.5.0 with the best and extreme presets under a 4-hour budget per run. Inputs: mean and std wind speed, Hs, Tp, and section height, radius and thickness; target: D1/3 (DEL format). This gives 82–96 models per tower for E1/E2 and 58–63 per fold for E3, plus seven standalone baselines (TabPFN, XGBoost, polynomial chaos, response surface, Gaussian process and others).

Metrics. Rel L² (relative L²-norm error) is the primary ranking metric; MRE (point-wise mean relative error) is the per-regime metric. Every leaderboard number is a bootstrap mean ± standard deviation over B = 2,000 resamples of whole operating conditions.

Results

E1 · Random split

Everything looks easy, the wrong models win

Under a random split, all top-10 surrogates appear strong: roughly 2–4% Rel L² DEL across towers, with overlapping confidence intervals between adjacent ranks. But the test set contains no hard regime: random validation leaves every extrapolation regime empty by construction. A condition-grouped random split is equally optimistic (0.0198 vs 0.0191 on REF), so the cause is absent extrapolation, not seed leakage.

A damage benchmark needs a regime-aware split, not a random one, to choose the right model.

E2 · Within tower

Different regimes pick different winners

The Global winner loses at the wind-and-wave extrapolation regime. EX_EX is the deepest extrapolation cell and has the highest error on every tower. The Global rank-1 WeightedEnsemble_L2 drops to EX_EX ranks 23 / 11 / 11 (REF / OPT1 / OPT2), while the EX_EX rank-1 NeuralNetFastAI_r102_BAG_L1 sits at Global ranks 79 / 73 / 69.

The crossover is robust and not an AutoML artifact. It holds for every partition setting that still leaves an extrapolation region, on all three towers, and after retraining the pool on two other training grids. TabPFN and tuned XGBoost do not beat the EX_EX winner. Selecting on validation leaves 1.5–1.9× the best available EX_EX error; the ensemble puts 86–88% of its weight on tree models, which under-predict beyond the training domain.

The crossover is a family effect. A tree-weighted ensemble wins globally and bagged networks win at EX_EX; the EX_EX top-10 is dominated by NeuralNet variants on all three towers, while TabM degrades most.

Wind drives the extrapolation. By model family, error grows 6–20× from In-train to Extrapolation on the wind axis, but only 1.1–1.4× on the wave axis.

Sections pick different winners. On REF no model wins both the base and the top; on OPT1/OPT2 the Global rank-1 keeps the base but loses section 30 to NeuralNetTorch_r22.

For damage prediction, the Global rank-1 does not remain best across regimes and sections: it loses at EX_EX and at the tower top.

E3 · Cross tower

The rank-1 fails again, on geometry and on sections

Transfer collapses on geometries far from training. Training on a set that includes REF generalises to the redesigned towers, with rank-1 Rel L² DEL of 0.067 (REF+OPT1 → OPT2) and 0.098 (REF+OPT2 → OPT1). Training without REF (OPT1+OPT2 → REF) reaches 0.423, a 4–6× increase: holding REF out under-predicts, consistent with its wider damage profile and most distinct geometry.

Sections pick different winners across folds. The section 1 winner is a NeuralNet variant on all three folds, while TabM_r52 wins section 30 on all three.

Scope and Future Work

Three geometries, one design family. The towers are the IEA-22 baseline and two fatigue-aware redesigns of it: the setting of design iteration, where a surrogate must follow the design as it changes. They are not near-duplicates: the first fore-aft frequency of REF lies close to the 3P excitation while the redesigns sit above it, and the base damage changes by a factor of about 40. E3 tests within-family transfer; cross-family generalisation (NREL 5 MW, DTU 10 MW, IEA-15) is left to future releases. The regime partition depends only on the wind/wave grid, so it carries over to other families.

Load case. The first release covers aligned wind–wave power production (DLC 1.2), the standard fatigue design load case of IEC 61400-3-2, which typically dominates lifetime tower fatigue. Misaligned, parked and idling cases involve different physics and new simulation campaigns.

Future work. Further tabular foundation models (TabICL, TabDPT), a time-series task on the ≈190 GB OpenFAST record (a 147-simulation audit subset is already released), other load cases and design families, and FEA/CFD meshes for multi-fidelity benchmarks.
